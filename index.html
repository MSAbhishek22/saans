Problem · Solution · Outcome

The problem

An AQI number on its own does not tell you when to go out. The air changes hour by hour, and the same air hurts a child, an elderly parent, or a person with asthma sooner than it hurts a healthy adult.

The solution

Saans fetches live air-quality data for your city and reads the forecast hour by hour. It picks the cleanest two-hour windows coming up, and the advice gets stricter for the people the air hurts most. The rules are short, written by hand, and shown in full under “How this works” below.

The outcome

You see the air right now, the best hours to step out today, the rest of today hour by hour, and a first look at tomorrow morning — for a run, for kids’ play, for an elderly parent’s walk, or for the commute. If no window is safe, Saans says so and shows the least-bad one.

    How this works

    1. Real data, live. Every number on this page is fetched at the moment you open it from the Open-Meteo Air Quality API, which serves the Copernicus CAMS forecast. There is no stored or made-up AQI anywhere in this app.
    2. Simple rules, not a black box. The planner looks at the forecast hour by hour and picks the cleanest two-hour windows coming up — into tomorrow morning if the day is nearly over. The advice comes from a short rule table, written by hand:
    When outdoor exercise is fine, by profile
    ProfileGo out if AQI isWhy
    General150 or underHealthy adults cope up to Unhealthy
    Child100 or underChildren breathe faster and are still growing
    Elderly100 or underOlder lungs and hearts feel it sooner
    Asthma / heart50 or underOnly Good air is safe to push in

    Built during EvoHacks 2, 21–22 Nov 2026. The whole app was written in the event window. AI helped draft code and wording, but a person wrote the rules, picked the data source, tested every screen, and can explain each line. No pre-existing code was reused.

    Forecast data comes from a weather model. For medical decisions, follow official CPCB readings and your doctor.
